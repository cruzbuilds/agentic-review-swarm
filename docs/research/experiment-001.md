# Experiment 001: Five Critics or One Good Prompt

**Question this answers.** On the same code, the same model, the same week: does the V1 swarm of
five narrow specialist reviewers find materially more real problems than one general reviewer, and
what does each miss? Secondary, registered before results: does the swarm produce an audit trail a
general review does not?

**What preceded it.** V1 in field use ([shelflife review log](https://github.com/cruzbuilds/shelflife/blob/main/docs/review-log.md):
37 findings, 0 overridden, 21 of 33 actionable ones the author would have missed) with no control
group. The claim "narrow charters beat one prompt" had never been tested against one prompt.

**Where the evidence lives.** In its own repository, on purpose:
[cruzbuilds/Five-Critics-or-One-Good-Prompt](https://github.com/cruzbuilds/Five-Critics-or-One-Good-Prompt).
That repository is the canonical record and is not duplicated here. It was kept separate because its
value depends on properties this repository cannot give it: predictions sealed by public commit
timestamp before any run, an append-only `findings/` directory enforced by a script, and a frozen
subject at one hash. The subject application is [cruzbuilds/idea-log](https://github.com/cruzbuilds/idea-log)
at `b38c5b0`. The swarm under test was this repository at
[`v1-experiment-final`](https://github.com/cruzbuilds/agentic-review-swarm/releases/tag/v1-experiment-final)
(`a18fba7`, tools condition; `7461982`, reading-only condition), recorded per run in
[`reports/runs.csv`](https://github.com/cruzbuilds/Five-Critics-or-One-Good-Prompt/blob/main/reports/runs.csv).

## Design, in brief

One application, generated by a coding agent from one paragraph in twelve and a half minutes,
committed as produced, frozen. Nobody reviewed it first; no defects were seeded. Three arms, same
model (Sonnet), three runs each:

- **A**, one general reviewer with a strong 170-word prompt naming eleven domains and asking for
  absences as well as defects. Registered.
- **B**, the V1 swarm. Registered.
- **C**, one general reviewer with twenty-four words and no domain map. Added after A and B were
  analysed, labeled post-hoc everywhere (D-005). No prediction is about it.

A and B ran twice: once with every tool silently denied by the author's harness (D-003, D-004; kept
as a labeled condition), once with tools working. Every claim from every run was checked against
the frozen code by one judge with open, versioned verdicts. There is no recall figure: nothing was
seeded, so each arm gets its share of the union of confirmed defects.

## What was measured

79 distinct claims after de-duplication; 64 confirmed under the strict rule (code matches and
nothing elsewhere prevents the failure).

| Prompt | 24 words | 170 words | five charters, contract, orchestrator |
| Distinct claims | 37 | 47 | 48 |
| Strict precision | 81% | 81% | 79% |
| Confirmed defects, all domains | 30 | 38 | 38 |
| Confirmed, the swarm's five domains | 18 | 24 | 35 |
| Same, without tests | 17 | 23 | 22 |
| Merge-blocking defects found, of 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 |
| Found in every run | 4 | 3 | 2 |
| Calibration defect (C26), as a finding | 3 of 3 | 5 of 6 | 0 of 6 |
| Missing test suite noticed | 3 of 3, one item | 6 of 6, one item | 6 of 6, thirteen items |
| Security | 11 | 11 | 7 |
| Correctness | 10 | 11 | 3 |
| Seen in all three runs (tools condition) | 60% | 44% | 66% |
| Report length, words | 877 to 1,199 | 2,176 to 2,901 | 1,708 to 2,136 |
| Per-agent record of what was and was not checked | no | no | every run |

Per domain, confirmed: tests 1 / 1 / 13; security 11 / 11 / 7; infrastructure 4 / 7 / 8;
documentation 1 / 2 / 5; dependencies 1 / 3 / 2; correctness 10 / 11 / 3; performance 2 / 2 / 0;
accessibility 0 / 1 / 0. Tools versus no tools, arms A and B: a handful of findings swapped each
way, one tool-dependent confirmed defect, already in the baseline scan.

Predictions, sealed before any run: (1) swarm wins every category, failed; (2) generalist misses a
critical the swarm catches, failed and the reverse happened; (3) biggest gap documentation then
security, half; (4) swarm precision higher, failed; (5) generalist does not notice the missing test
suite, failed six of six, then three of three for arm C.

## What the evidence supports

- On this subject the specialists were capable reviewers: same precision as both generalists, the
  most repeatable arm, the only decomposition of "no tests" into a work list, the only inspection
  record.
- The architecture had an ownership problem. The most consequential defect in the subject,
  predictions rewritable after the outcome is recorded, was noticed by `test-reviewer` in two of six
  swarm runs and placed under "Handoffs nobody picked up: no agent in the roster owns this." It never
  received a severity and never affected a verdict. Both generalists reported it as a finding.
- Both generalists out-found the security specialist inside its own lane, 11 and 11 to 7, once with
  a prompt that named security and once without. Cause not established.
- Most of the high-value capability was in the model: twenty-four words found every merge-blocking
  defect every run. The engineered prompt added hygiene breadth and cost consistency.

## What the evidence does not support

- That five critics beat one good prompt. They tied one and beat the other by eight hygiene items.
  The repository's original framing, "so far the log says they do," was wrong and has been retired.
- That specialization improves defect coverage in general. One specialist improved one domain.
- Anything about cost or latency (A and B were not instrumented), tools (the subject had nothing for
  most of them to run against), or statistics (three runs describe stability).
- Anything beyond this subject, which was the kind V1 was least built for: a whole freshly generated
  repository with no engagement documents, no tests, no CI, almost no infrastructure.

Limitations, stated in full in the analysis: one repository, one model family, one week, one author
who built one arm and judged all three; the second judge the protocol registered has not run; the
strong prompt was written by the swarm's author; arm C is one naive prompt written by someone who had
seen the results.

## What came next

The reading that motivated V2: the specialists were not the problem; the architecture had no owner
for defects that emerge between reasonable local operations, and its merge did not reason about
provenance, corroboration, disagreement, coverage, or unowned findings.
[ADR 0006](../decisions/0006-systems-review-beside-the-specialists.md), then
[V2](v2-hybrid-review.md).

**A note on numbering.** `ANALYSIS.md` section 13.6 and the paper's section 12 describe a future
"Experiment 002" that would compare V1 against V2 on a mature subject. That experiment has not run.
The V2 evaluation runs recorded in [v2-hybrid-review.md](v2-hybrid-review.md) are component and
integration evaluations of V2's mechanisms, not that comparison, and are not called Experiment 002
anywhere in this repository to keep the two apart.
